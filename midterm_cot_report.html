What the midterm history says

  • Post-midterm strength is real but small-sample. NQ, ES and gold were higher one year after all 5 midterms with price data; silver and gold were higher 60 days after all 5.
  • COT Bearish setups on NQ/ES at midterms didn't pay. NQ was a Bearish setup in 2006 and 2010 and ES in 2006, and all three were higher 60 days and a year later. The seasonal tailwind overpowered the positioning read. That's another reason not to force a bearish NQ lean from the Legacy view right now.
  • COT Bullish setups at midterms did pay. NQ 2022 (+16% in 60 days), gold 2006, 2018 and 2022 (all up 5-9% in 60 days), silver 2014 (+12%).
  • 2018 is the exception, and it's the Trump analog. An October selloff, a bounce into the vote, then a second leg: NQ fell from 7,200 on election day to 5,802 on Christmas Eve (-19.4%, -24.7% from the high), ES -17.7%. Metals barely dipped (silver -4.6%, gold -2.5%) and were up 10% and 8% two months later. BTC, a Bearish setup that week, lost 47% in 60 days.

  • Gold speculators have been more crowded long under Trump's second term than under any other president (47% of open interest on average vs 25-38% before). That crowding is what's unwinding now: gold is off 31% from January's high.
  • Silver has spent 57% of this term in a Legacy Bullish setup, versus about 20% in every prior term, yet it's down 60% from its January peak. Commercials have been leaning long into a falling market for months. That's a reminder that a COT setup is a lean for v7 signals, not a timing tool.
  • NQ positioning is ordinary for a Trump term (8% vs 8.7% in the first term). The crowding in NQ is concentrated in hedge funds (dashboard 86) and retail (75), not in the broad speculator group.
  • Party or president, on its own, doesn't predict COT direction in this data. Every term had both setups. The useful part is the midterm calendar and the size of the current unwinds.
